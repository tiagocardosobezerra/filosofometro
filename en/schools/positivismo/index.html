Positivism and scientism

A tradition that takes science as the model for all reliable knowledge and wants to apply scientific method to society too.

What it is

Positivism holds that we truly know only what can be observed, tested and organized into laws, as science does. Metaphysical speculation about ultimate causes would be useless. Its core question is: how can we gain secure knowledge and use it to improve human life? Scientism, in a broad sense, extends this confidence: philosophy should work alongside the sciences, using their results and their standards of rigor, instead of competing with them.

Auguste Comte created positivism in 19th-century France. He proposed the law of three stages, according to which thought moves from the religious to the metaphysical and finally to the scientific, and he helped found sociology. His ideas left a strong mark on Brazil: the motto Order and Progress on its flag comes from him. In the 20th century, the Argentine Mario Bunge criticized classical positivism but defended a scientific, realist and materialist philosophy, and fought what he considered pseudoscience.

Core ideas

  • Reliable knowledge comes from observation and the scientific method.
  • Society, like nature, can be studied in terms of laws.
  • Philosophy should work alongside the sciences, not against them.
