What it is

Liberation philosophy and theology think from the standpoint of the poor and excluded of Latin America. For them, it is not enough to reflect on justice in the abstract: one must start from the concrete reality of oppression and commit to overcoming it. The core question is: how can thought, education and faith help the oppressed become agents of their own history, rather than mere objects of other people's help?

The movement gained strength in the 1960s and 1970s, amid the inequalities and dictatorships of the continent and after the Second Vatican Council. Paulo Freire proposed a pedagogy in which learners grow by reading their own world critically. The Peruvian Gustavo Gutiérrez and the Brazilian Leonardo Boff formulated liberation theology, with its preferential option for the poor. The Argentine Enrique Dussel developed an ethics and a philosophy from the standpoint of victims and the world's periphery. Their ideas remain alive in education and social movements.

Core ideas

  • Thought should start from the concrete reality of the poor and excluded.
  • To educate is to help people read their own world critically.
  • Faith and ethics demand a commitment to social justice.
