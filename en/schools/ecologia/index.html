What it is

This group questions the idea that human beings are separate from nature and above it. Rivers, forests, animals and mountains would not be mere resources but part of a web of life with value of its own, to which we also belong. The core question is: how can we live on Earth without destroying it, and what can other cultures, especially Indigenous peoples, teach us about living together with it?

The Norwegian Arne Naess founded deep ecology in the 1970s, which recognizes value in every form of life and calls for changes in how we live, not just technical fixes. In Brazil, Ailton Krenak, a leader of the Krenak people, criticizes the idea of a humanity separate from the Earth. The anthropologist Eduardo Viveiros de Castro described Amerindian perspectivism, in which animals and spirits also see themselves as people. In the face of the climate crisis, these voices have drawn wide attention.

Core ideas

  • Nature has value in itself, not only as a human resource.
  • Human beings are part of the Earth, not above it.
  • Indigenous knowledge offers other ways of understanding nature.
