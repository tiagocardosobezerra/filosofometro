What it is

Cynics and Stoics ask what truly depends on us and what we need in order to live well. Their shared answer: virtue is enough. Wealth, fame and comfort are not the true good, and whoever clings to them becomes a slave of chance. The Cynics took this to the extreme, living with almost nothing and defying convention. The Stoics turned it into a way of life: accept what we cannot control and act justly in what we can.

Cynicism arose in Athens in the fourth century BC with Antisthenes and Diogenes, famous for living in a jar and mocking the powerful. Soon after, Zeno of Citium founded Stoicism, inspired by that simplicity but with a broader view of the cosmos and civic duty. In Rome the school gained three famous voices: Seneca, adviser to Nero; Epictetus, a former slave; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. Today their ideas reappear in self-help books and in cognitive psychology.

Core ideas

  • Virtue is the only true good
  • We must separate what depends on us from what does not
  • To live well is to live in agreement with nature and reason
