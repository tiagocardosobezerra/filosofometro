What it is

The Enlightenment held that people should use their own reason instead of accepting, without examination, the authority of kings, churches and customs. Its core question was: how can life and politics be organized rationally, securing freedom and dignity for every individual? From this came the defense of religious tolerance, freedom of expression, broad education, natural rights and governments that answer to their citizens.

The movement gained strength in 18th-century Europe, above all in France, England and Scotland, and inspired the American and French revolutions. Voltaire was its most famous voice against intolerance and fanaticism. Thomas Paine brought these ideas to a wide public, defending American independence and the rights of man. In Spanish America, Andrés Bello applied this spirit to the education and law of the new republics. Many of today's democracies inherit these ideals.

Core ideas

  • Reason should examine every authority, including religious and political ones.
  • Every human being has natural rights that government must respect.
  • Education and science are paths to progress and freedom.
