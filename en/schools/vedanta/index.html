What it is

Vedanta asks what ultimate reality is and who we truly are. Drawing on the Upanishads, the texts that close the Vedas, it holds that there is an absolute reality, Brahman, and a deep self, Atman. In its Advaita, or non-dual, branch the two are one and the same: the feeling of being individuals cut off from the whole comes from ignorance, and liberation comes from directly knowing this unity.

Vedanta took shape over centuries in India through commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras and the Bhagavad Gita. Around the eighth century, Shankara gave Advaita Vedanta its classic form, travelling across India, debating other schools and founding monasteries. Later branches kept some distinction between the self and God. In the nineteenth century Vedanta reached the West and influenced writers, scientists and spiritual seekers.

Core ideas

  • There is a single ultimate reality, called Brahman
  • The deep self (Atman) is identical to Brahman
  • Liberation comes from knowledge, not from ritual alone
