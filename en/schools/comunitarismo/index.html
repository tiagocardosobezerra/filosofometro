Communitarianism and republicanism

Traditions that stress community, virtue and civic participation in response to individualism.

What it is

This tradition criticizes the image of the isolated individual who chooses everything alone. For it, we are formed by communities, languages, traditions and shared practices, and the good life depends on taking part in them. Republicanism adds that freedom is realized in public action, when citizens deliberate together about their common world. The core question is: what binds us to one another, and what do we owe to the political community?

Hannah Arendt, who lived through totalitarianism, defended politics as a space of action and speech among equals. In the 1980s, Alasdair MacIntyre and Charles Taylor criticized individualist liberalism: MacIntyre proposed reviving Aristotle's ethics of virtue within living traditions, and Taylor showed how identity depends on recognition by others. None of them accepted every label, but their ideas remain central to debates on citizenship, multiculturalism and social cohesion.

Core ideas

  • Personal identity is formed within communities and traditions.
  • Freedom is realized through active participation in public life.
  • Virtues and common goods are essential to a good society.
