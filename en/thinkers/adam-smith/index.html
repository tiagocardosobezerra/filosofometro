Who was Adam Smith

A professor of moral philosophy in Glasgow, Adam Smith is remembered as the father of modern economics. In The Wealth of Nations, he explained how the division of labor and free exchange generate prosperity. Before that, he wrote about sympathy as the basis of morality, showing that his economic thought had ethical roots.

Core ideas

  • When each person pursues their own interest, the market tends to generate wealth for all
  • The division of labor multiplies a society's productivity
  • Morality arises from sympathy: we put ourselves in another's place to judge

The tradition: Economic liberalism

Economic liberalism defends free markets, property and little state interference in the economy. Adam Smith is its classical founder, with the idea that free exchange organizes society.

In the same school: Friedrich Hayek, John Rawls, Isaiah Berlin, Martha Nussbaum, Kwame Anthony Appiah
