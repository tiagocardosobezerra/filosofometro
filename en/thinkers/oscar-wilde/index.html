Who was Oscar Wilde

An Irish writer, Wilde was the most brilliant figure of the movement that defended art for art's sake. In novels, plays, and essays, he celebrated beauty, pleasure, and individual freedom against Victorian morality. Sentenced to prison for his homosexuality, he died poor in Paris, a few years after leaving jail.

Core ideas

  • Art has no moral obligation: its value lies in beauty and the intensity it provokes
  • To live is to make one's own life a work of art, cultivating experiences and sensations
  • Socialism is worthwhile only if it frees the individual to develop their personality fully

The tradition: Aestheticism

Aestheticism places beauty and intense experience above usefulness and conventional morality. Oscar Wilde is its most famous spokesman, who made his own life and work a manifesto of this idea.

In the same school: Aristippus, Epicurus, Lucretius, Michel Onfray
