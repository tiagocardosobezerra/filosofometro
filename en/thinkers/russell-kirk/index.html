Who was Russell Kirk

An American historian and essayist, Kirk helped found modern conservatism in the United States with a 1953 book on the legacy of Edmund Burke. He lived in a small Michigan town, far from academic centers. He defended a transcendent moral order, inherited customs, and local communities.

Core ideas

  • There is an enduring moral order, transcendent in origin, that society must respect
  • Inherited customs, conventions, and institutions hold wisdom accumulated over generations
  • Local communities and social variety are worth more than uniform projects of the central government

The tradition: Traditionalist conservatism

Traditionalist conservatism defends religion, customs, and inherited institutions as the basis of a good society. Russell Kirk gave this line its classic formulation in the United States.

In the same school: Edmund Burke, Joseph de Maistre, Michael Oakeshott, Roger Scruton, Thomas Sowell
