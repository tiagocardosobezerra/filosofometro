Who was Iris Murdoch

Born in Dublin and raised in London, Iris Murdoch taught philosophy at Oxford and wrote twenty six novels, winning the Booker Prize for The Sea, the Sea. Against the fashion of her time, she defended an almost Platonic idea of the Good. For her, moral life happens less in big choices than in the quiet effort to see other people justly and lovingly.

Core ideas

  • Morality is mostly about attention: learning to see others as they really are
  • The Good is real and draws us out of our selfish fantasies
  • Art and love both teach us to look beyond ourselves

The tradition: Moral Platonist

Moral Platonism treats the Good as something real that we discover, not something we invent. Murdoch revived this view in the twentieth century against existentialism and analytic skepticism.

In the same school: Socrates, Plato, Plotinus, Saint Augustine, Ralph Cudworth
