Who was Shankara

Adi Shankara was an Indian philosopher and monk who, according to tradition, lived just over 30 years and traveled across India debating other schools. He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita and organized monasteries that still exist. He is the greatest systematizer of Advaita Vedanta, one of the most influential philosophies of Hinduism.

Core ideas

  • Only Brahman, the absolute reality, is fully real
  • The deep self, the Atman, is identical to Brahman
  • The world of differences is an appearance caused by ignorance, called maya

The tradition: Advaita Vedanta

Advaita Vedanta means non-dual Vedanta: the Hindu school that teaches that everything is, at bottom, a single reality. Shankara is its main systematizer and greatest reference.
