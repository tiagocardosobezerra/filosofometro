Who was Henry Sidgwick

A professor at Cambridge, Sidgwick wrote the most rigorous work of classical utilitarianism, carefully comparing the moral theories of his day. He argued that basic ethical principles are known by reason and lead us to seek the greatest general happiness. He also helped open the university to women and studied psychic phenomena.

Core ideas

  • The good to be promoted is the greatest sum of happiness, counting everyone's equally
  • Some moral principles are self-evident to reason, such as impartiality between persons
  • The conflict between self-interest and the general good is a problem that reason does not resolve

The tradition: Utilitarianism

Utilitarianism judges actions by their consequences for the happiness of all. Sidgwick is its most rigorous systematizer, who sought to ground the principle of utility in rational intuitions.

In the same school: Jeremy Bentham, John Stuart Mill, Peter Singer
