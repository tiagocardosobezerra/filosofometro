Who was C. L. R. James

A schoolteacher and cricket writer from Trinidad, James moved to England in 1932 and became a leading voice for Caribbean and African independence. His history of the Haitian Revolution, The Black Jacobins, showed enslaved people as makers of their own history. A dissident Marxist, he trusted ordinary workers to organize themselves more than any party, and he wrote about sport as a mirror of society.

Core ideas

  • The oppressed make their own history; they are not just its victims
  • Workers can govern themselves without a ruling party above them
  • Culture, even cricket, reveals the deepest conflicts of a society

The tradition: Caribbean Marxist

Caribbean Marxism links the critique of capitalism to the struggle against colonialism and slavery. James is its great historian and a defender of self-organization from below.

In the same school: Charles Fourier, Karl Marx, Antonio Gramsci, Slavoj Žižek
