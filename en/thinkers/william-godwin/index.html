Who was William Godwin

A former dissenting minister, Godwin published a work in 1793 that made him the first great theoretical defender of anarchism. He argued that reason and impartial benevolence would make government unnecessary. He was married to Mary Wollstonecraft and was the father of Mary Shelley, author of Frankenstein.

Core ideas

  • The right action is the one that produces the greatest good, even if it goes against family ties or promises
  • Government is an evil that the progress of reason will make dispensable
  • Human actions are determined by causes, so education and debate can change them

The tradition: Utilitarian anarchism

Utilitarian anarchism rejects the State because impartial reason, aimed at the general good, is enough to guide common life. Godwin founded this line and defended it by persuasion, not violence.

In the same school: Max Stirner, Proudhon, Mikhail Bakunin, Emma Goldman, Noam Chomsky
