Who was Wittgenstein

Ludwig Wittgenstein was born into one of the richest families in Vienna and studied with Russell at Cambridge. With the Tractatus, he believed he had solved the problems of philosophy and went off to teach primary school in rural Austria. Years later he returned to Cambridge and changed his mind, showing that the meaning of words lies in their use.

Core ideas

  • Many philosophical problems arise from confusions in the use of language
  • The meaning of a word is its use within a language game
  • Language is tied to forms of life shared by a community

The tradition: Philosophy of language

The philosophy of language investigates how words acquire meaning and how language connects to the world and to thought. Wittgenstein transformed this field twice, with very different views.

In the same school: Gottlob Frege, Bertrand Russell, Karl Popper
