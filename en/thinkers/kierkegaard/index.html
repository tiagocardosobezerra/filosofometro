Who was Kierkegaard

Søren Kierkegaard was a Danish thinker who wrote under several pseudonyms and criticized his country's official Church. For him, faith is not a set of doctrines but a personal and anguished choice before God. He is considered the father of existentialism for placing the concrete individual at the center of philosophy.

Core ideas

  • The truth that matters is subjective, the one by which one lives and dies
  • Anxiety arises from awareness of freedom and of possibilities
  • Faith is a personal leap that goes beyond reason and demands risk

The tradition: Christian existentialist

Christian existentialism focuses on individual existence, choice and anxiety, understood within a personal relationship with God. Kierkegaard is its founder and main reference.

In the same school: Nietzsche, Jean-Paul Sartre, Albert Camus
