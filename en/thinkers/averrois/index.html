Who was Averroes

Ibn Rushd, known in the West as Averroes, was a judge, physician and philosopher in Muslim Córdoba. He wrote commentaries on Aristotle so thorough that Europeans simply called him The Commentator. He argued that philosophy and religion do not contradict each other, and his ideas stirred up the medieval Christian universities.

Core ideas

  • Philosophy and religion seek the same truth and cannot contradict each other
  • Those who are capable have a duty to study philosophy and use reason
  • Aristotle's work is the highest point of human knowledge

The tradition: Aristotelian (falsafa)

Falsafa is the Arabic name for Greek-inspired philosophy in the Islamic world, and Aristotelian means one who follows Aristotle. Averroes is the greatest Aristotelian of this tradition.

In the same school: Aristotle, Maimonides, Thomas Aquinas, William of Ockham, Elizabeth Anscombe
