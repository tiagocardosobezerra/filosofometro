Who was Thomas Hobbes

Thomas Hobbes lived through the English Civil War and spent years in exile in Paris. In Leviathan, he argued that without a strong government, human life would be a war of all against all. His theory of the social contract, based on fear and the search for security, founded modern political philosophy.

Core ideas

  • Without a state, human life would be solitary, poor, brutal and short
  • People make a contract and hand power to a sovereign in exchange for security
  • The sovereign needs strong, indivisible power to prevent civil war

The tradition: Contractarian

A contractarian explains the state as the result of an agreement among people. Hobbes is the first great modern contractarian and defends a strong sovereign.
