Who was Albert Camus

Born in Algeria to a poor family, Albert Camus was a journalist and novelist and took part in the French Resistance. In The Stranger and The Myth of Sisyphus, he explored the clash between our search for meaning and a silent world. He won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1957 and died in a car accident at 46.

Core ideas

  • The absurd arises from the clash between our thirst for meaning and the silence of the world
  • The answer to the absurd is neither suicide nor escape, but lucid revolt
  • No cause justifies killing innocents in the name of an ideal future

The tradition: Absurdist

Absurdism sees life as a conflict between the human search for meaning and a universe that does not answer. Camus is its leading representative and proposes living fully in spite of it.

In the same school: Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Jean-Paul Sartre
