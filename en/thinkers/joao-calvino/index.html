Who was John Calvin

Jean Calvin was born in France, studied law and joined the Protestant Reformation, having to flee the country. In Geneva, he organized a church and a city according to strict biblical principles. His Institutes became the foundation of Reformed Protestantism, which spread through Switzerland, the Netherlands, Scotland and North America.

Core ideas

  • God is sovereign over everything, and only his grace saves human beings
  • God chose from eternity who will be saved, known as predestination
  • The Bible is the highest authority in matters of faith, above tradition

The tradition: Reformer

Reformer refers to the leaders of the 16th-century Protestant Reformation, who broke with Rome. Calvin is one of the main ones, founder of the Reformed or Calvinist tradition.

In the same school: Pascal, G. K. Chesterton, Simone Weil, C. S. Lewis, John Henry Newman
