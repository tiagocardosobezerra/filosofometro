Who is Peter Singer

An Australian philosopher who taught for many years at Princeton University. His book Animal Liberation, from 1975, gave a boost to the animal rights movement. He argues that we should take into account the suffering of every being capable of feeling it, and he is one of the inspirations of the effective altruism movement.

Core ideas

  • The suffering of any sentient being counts, whether human or animal
  • Whoever can help prevent great suffering at little cost has a duty to help
  • Discriminating against beings because of their species is a prejudice similar to racism

The tradition: Contemporary utilitarian

Contemporary utilitarianism judges actions by their consequences, aiming to reduce suffering and increase the well-being of all. Singer is today its best-known representative.

In the same school: Jeremy Bentham, John Stuart Mill, Henry Sidgwick
