Who was Alan Watts

Fascinated by Buddhism as a teenager in England, Watts moved to the United States, served briefly as an Episcopal priest and then settled in California. Through books and radio talks he became the best known interpreter of Zen and Taoism for Western audiences. Witty and unconventional, he became a guide for the counterculture of the 1960s, and his recorded lectures still circulate widely.

Core ideas

  • The separate ego is an illusion; we are expressions of the whole universe
  • Clinging to security makes us anxious; accepting change sets us free
  • Life is more like music or dance than a journey toward a goal

The tradition: Zen popularizer

Zen popularization brought Buddhist and Taoist ideas to a broad Western public, often mixed with psychology. Watts is its most charismatic voice, more a teacher of attitudes than a builder of systems.
