Who was Claude Lévi-Strauss

A French anthropologist and philosopher who taught at the newly founded University of São Paulo in the 1930s and studied Indigenous peoples of Brazil. He founded structural anthropology, searching for the hidden rules behind myths, kinship, and customs. His book Tristes Tropiques blends travel narrative with reflection on the place of human beings in the world.

Core ideas

  • Different cultures follow deep, shared mental structures
  • Myths and kinship rules work as systems, much like a language
  • The thought of so-called primitive peoples is as logical as scientific thought

The tradition: Structuralist

Structuralism looks for invisible structures, such as rules and oppositions, that organize language, culture, and society. Lévi-Strauss took this method from linguistics into anthropology and made it famous.
