Who was C. S. Lewis

A scholar of medieval literature at Oxford and later Cambridge, Lewis lost his childhood faith and found it again in his thirties, partly through talks with his friend J. R. R. Tolkien. His wartime radio talks on Christianity became Mere Christianity, and his Narnia books reached millions of children. He wrote with unusual clarity about pain, love, grief and the moral law.

Core ideas

  • There is a moral law that all cultures recognize and none invented
  • Our desire for joy points to something beyond this world
  • Reducing values to feelings would abolish what makes us human

The tradition: Christian apologist

Christian apologetics is the reasoned defense of the faith for a skeptical audience. Lewis is its most popular modern practitioner, arguing from common experience rather than from theology.
