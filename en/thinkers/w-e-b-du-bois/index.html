Who was W. E. B. Du Bois

The first Black American to earn a doctorate from Harvard, Du Bois was a sociologist, historian, novelist and editor. He helped found the NAACP, edited its magazine The Crisis for decades, organized Pan-African congresses and studied Black life with scientific rigor. Late in life he embraced socialism, moved to Ghana and died there on the eve of the March on Washington.

Core ideas

  • Black Americans live with a double consciousness, seeing themselves through others' eyes
  • Racism is a system that needs to be studied, named and fought
  • People of African descent around the world share a common cause

The tradition: Pan-Africanist

Pan-Africanism is the idea that peoples of African descent share a history and a future and should unite against colonialism and racism. Du Bois is one of its founders and its greatest scholar.

In the same school: Frederick Douglass, B. R. Ambedkar, Martin Luther King Jr., Frantz Fanon
